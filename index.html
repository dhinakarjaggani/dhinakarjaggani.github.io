    5Case files

    How I handle real building situations.

    Tap a case to open it.

    An elevator stops with a resident insideEmergency response, by the book

    The situation

    An elevator stopped between floors with a resident inside. Keep them calm and safe, get the right people moving, and keep the building informed.

    What I did

    Stayed in contact with the resident, called the elevator company's emergency line, notified the superintendent and property manager, updated residents, and recorded the full timeline.

    The result

    Handled with the right people involved, and the property manager had a complete written record to follow up with the contractor.

    A complaint that keeps coming backTurning it into a clear record for management

    The situation

    A resident raised the same concern again and again. Not every issue can be fixed on the spot, and scattered notes make decisions hard.

    What I did

    Listened, explained what could and couldn't be done right away, documented each report with dates and details, and gave the property manager one clear summary.

    The result

    The resident knew they were taken seriously, and management had the full history in one place to act on.

    Contractors in a full-amenity towerAccess, notices and follow-through

    The situation

    A tower with a pool, sauna and fitness centre has contractors in and out all year. Every visit needs approval, and residents need notice of closures.

    What I did

    Confirmed approvals with the property manager first, coordinated timing with the superintendent, posted closure notices, and logged arrivals, departures and issues.

    The result

    Contractors only worked with approval, residents weren't caught off guard, and incomplete work was flagged quickly.

    Nothing lost between shiftsBuilding a reliable handover

    The situation

    In a building that runs around the clock, open items change hands every shift. When handovers rely on memory, residents end up asking twice.

    What I did

    Made sure every open item was written down with what happened, who it was passed to and what was pending, and trained the team to the same standard.

    The result

    Follow-ups became consistent across shifts, and the management office could see what had been reported without chasing updates.
