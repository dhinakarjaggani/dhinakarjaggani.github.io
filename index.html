How I handle real building situations.

Case studies

Each case reflects the kind of situation I handle on site. Details have been generalized so no building, resident or company is identifiable.

Building a reliable handover and follow-up process

The story

In a building that runs around the clock, open items change hands at every shift: contractors expected later, residents waiting on an answer, requests still in progress. Follow-through depends on what gets written down.

Challenge

When handovers rely on memory or a quick verbal update, items slip and residents end up asking twice.

What I did

Made sure every open item was recorded at handover with what happened, who it was passed to and what was still pending, and walked the team through the same standard.

Result

Follow-ups became consistent across shifts, and the property management office could see what had been reported without chasing for updates.

Managing an emergency by the book

The story

Emergencies in a high-rise are rare, but the first response sets the tone. An elevator stopped between floors with a resident inside.

Challenge

Keep the resident calm and safe, get the right people moving fast, keep the rest of the building informed, and leave a clear record for management.

What I did

Stayed in contact with the resident, called the elevator company's emergency line, notified the superintendent and property manager, updated other residents on the outage, and recorded the full timeline in an incident report.

Result

The situation was handled with the right people involved, and the property manager had a complete written record to follow up with the elevator contractor.

Turning a recurring complaint into a clear record for management

The story

Some resident concerns come back again and again. Each time, the resident needs to feel heard and the office needs enough detail to act.

Challenge

Not every issue can be solved on the spot, and scattered notes make it hard for management to make a decision.

What I did

Listened, explained what could and couldn't be done right away, documented each report with dates and details, and gave the property manager one clear summary.

Result

The resident knew the concern was being taken seriously, and management had the full history in one place to act on.

Coordinating contractors in a full-amenity building

The story

A tower with a pool, sauna, hot tub, fitness centre, running track and yoga room has contractors in and out all year: scheduled service, inspections, maintenance and seasonal work.

Challenge

Every visit needs approved access, residents need notice of amenity closures, and work has to be completed, not just started.

What I did

Confirmed access approvals with the property manager first, coordinated timing with the superintendent, posted notices for closures, and logged arrivals, departures and any issues.

Result

Contractors only worked with proper approval, residents weren't caught off guard, and incomplete or problem work was flagged to management quickly.
